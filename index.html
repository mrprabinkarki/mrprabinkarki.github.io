01 — About

Structural biologist, trained as a physicist.

I'm a PhD candidate in Physics (biophysics) at the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee, in the lab of Prof. Marius Schmidt. I have over three years of hands-on experience determining macromolecular structures, from protein expression and purification through data processing to model building.

My research centres on bacteriophytochromes: how they change shape when they absorb light, and how they pass that signal on to their partner proteins. I lead the project on the phytochrome–response regulator complex.

I'm looking for postdoctoral positions in structural biology and drug discovery, and I'm open to relocating.

02 — Research

How does a protein turn a photon into a signal?

I approach this question from the structure side.

Phytochrome signaling complexes

Cryo-EM structures of a full-length phytochrome histidine kinase bound to its response regulator. Light loosens the complex, so it controls signaling through both kinase activity and reversible binding.

Time-resolved cryo-EM

Illuminating particles on the grid and vitrifying them 10 ms later with the Spotiton technique. This captured a Pr/Pfr heterodimer and a 180° rotation of the kinase domain.

Time-resolved crystallography

Serial crystallography at synchrotrons and X-ray free-electron lasers to follow structural changes in photoreceptors and enzymes, with groups in the US and Europe.
